Build. Fly. Compete.

NeuroFly is a hands-on learning platform for high-school students (grades 9-12) and first-year university students. You fabricate a 45-gram indoor quadcopter and its handheld transmitter, program the flight controller in embedded C++, tune it in a browser simulator, and fly it through gates. A NeuroFly Grand Prix competition is planned for later.

Who it is for

One drone, five disciplines

Designed for ages roughly 14 to 19: grades 9-12 and first-year university. No drone experience is needed. A teacher or instructor guides the build; the guides in this repository are written to be followed step by step.

  • Digital fabrication
  • Electronics and soldering
  • Embedded C++
  • Control theory and PID
  • Computer vision

Path 1 · Build

Make the hardware

Turn files into a flying machine.

  • Laser-cut the airframe, 3D-print flexible TPU prop ducts
  • Solder surface-mount parts onto the carrier board
  • Read a schematic and a KiCad PCB layout
  • Budget mass: every gram counts

Path 2 · Fly

Pilot and tune

Learn how a small drone really behaves.

  • Practise in the browser simulator before any real flight
  • Arm safely, fly gates, understand the failsafe
  • Change PID gains and watch stability change
  • Follow the safety rules: ducts, eyewear, emergency cut

Path 3 · Code

Program the machine

From first sketch to autonomous behavior.

  • Embedded C++ on the XIAO ESP32-C6
  • Radio link with ESP-NOW, packet checksums
  • PID attitude control from IMU data
  • State machines with a ToF sensor; ArUco marker tracking in Python

The Kit Box

Meet the Kit Box

Everything one team needs to build the drone and its transmitter: boards, raw SMD parts, motors, laser-cut and 3D-printed pieces. Nothing arrives pre-assembled, so students build, solder, inspect and calibrate it themselves.

Open the interactive Kit Box Packing guide
  • Boards: drone carrier PCB (36 x 36 mm) and transmitter PCB (85 x 60 mm)
  • Fabrication: birch airframe spine and four TPU prop ducts
  • Compute: two XIAO ESP32-C6 modules
  • Power: four coreless motors, MOSFETs, diodes, 1S LiPo

Airframe and duct physics

A laser-cut birch or acrylic spine (85 mm diagonal) carries four motors, each inside a 3D-printed flexible TPU duct.

  • Center of gravity: kept central, with the LiPo cradled under the frame.
  • Weight budget: all-up weight target under 45 g including the battery.
  • Safety: soft ducts enclose every propeller. Powering motors without them is not allowed.
Frame DXF Duct STL
X-configuration, nose up

Two motors spin clockwise and two counter-clockwise so yaw torque cancels.

Carrier board and power switching

Made on a FabLab PCB mill (Roland SRM-20) or ordered from a PCB service.

  • Processor: Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C6, 32-bit RISC-V at 160 MHz.
  • Motor drivers: four AO3400A N-channel MOSFETs, 100 Ω gate resistors, 10 kΩ pull-downs.
  • Protection: four SS14 Schottky flyback diodes absorb back-EMF spikes.
  • Sensors: MPU6050 IMU over I2C; optional VL53L0X or VL53L1X time-of-flight ranger.

Competition Coming soon

NeuroFly Grand Prix: planned

No date, venue or entry process has been announced. This is the proposed format so teachers and students can prepare. Details may change.

Planned division

High School (grades 9-12)

Teams score on all three pillars. Fabrication and piloting carry most of the points that are within reach; the autonomous missions are optional stretch goals for teams with the programming experience.

Planned division

University, year 1

Same kit, same arena, same pillars, scored separately from high school. Teams are expected to attempt the autonomous and vision missions.

Curriculum

A four-week workshop

One way to run it with a class. Adapt the pace to your timetable.

Week 1

Digital fabrication

Laser-cut airframes with kerf adjustment, 3D-print flexible TPU prop ducts, budget mass under 45 g.

Week 2

Electronics

SMD soldering (0805 passives, SOT-23 MOSFETs), PCB milling on the SRM-20, short-circuit checks.

Week 2 guide

Week 3

Firmware and simulator

ESP-NOW link, transmitter with OLED, and flying the 3D simulator with your own controller.

Week 3 guide

Week 4

Flight and tuning

PID tuning, gate flying, altitude hold and vision-based landing, as a practice round.
